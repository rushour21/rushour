Rushour

A personal execution system. It does not help you plan more — it makes you plan less and finish what you planned, then learns how much you can actually do.

North star
Plan-completion ≥70% in week 4, ambition unchanged
v1 surface
Day Session, Goals, Plan, Review — 4 screens
AI budget
5 call sites, ~$0.04 / user / month
Time budget
Clock in 90s · out 60s · weekly 5m

01Thesis & principles

The failure being solved is not missing information. It is too many competing priorities, plans written for a person with more hours than the user has, and no feedback that connects the two.

So the product is a loop, and every screen exists to advance it:

Clock in→Choose the outcome→Capacity check→Execute→Clock out→Diagnose→Adjust next plan↵

Seven principles that bind every decision below

  1. Arithmetic is code, never a model. Capacity, calibration, readiness, drift and ranking are pure functions. They must be reproducible and explainable on demand.
  2. A plan is a record, not a view. Plans are written once and never mutated. The product's entire value is the measured gap between planned and actual; a mutable plan deletes its own evidence.
  3. Admission control is a write constraint. Two active goals maximum, enforced in the data layer. A limit that lives in the UI is a suggestion, and every user overrides it in week one.
  4. The error is the plan, not the person. Incomplete work is never red. Overage is attributed to the plan that was too big.
  5. Every field names the decision it changes. If a datum alters no recommendation, it is not collected. This deletes most of a conventional health tracker.
  6. Screens have time budgets. Exceeding one is a bug with the same severity as a crash.
  7. Cold start is designed, not endured. Day one uses borrowed priors and shrinks toward personal data as it arrives.

Measurement

One north star: plan-completion rate ≥70% by week 4 with stated ambition unchanged. Ambition must be held constant, or the metric is gamed by planning nothing.

Three counter-metrics that must not degrade:

  • Time in app trends down week over week after week 2.
  • Active goal count stays at ≤2 without the user fighting the constraint.
  • Planned tasks per day converges toward observed completion, not upward.

02The Day Session

Clock in and clock out are not two buttons. They are the boundary object the whole system hangs off — the unit of planning, the unit of measurement, and the unit of review.

Introducing it solves four problems that are otherwise unsolved:

  • The day boundary. "Did I do it today?" becomes a question about a session, not a UTC range. Travel and DST stop mattering.
  • Actuals without logging. Nobody reliably records how long things took. A session with one active task at a time derives duration for free.
  • Real available time. Capacity stops being a self-reported estimate and becomes clock-in to planned clock-out, minus committed blocks — an observed window.
  • A trigger for rescue. Rescue mode fires on remaining session time, not on an arbitrary 7 PM cron.

Lifecycle

NONE→ clock in →OPEN→ pause →PAUSED→ resume →OPEN→ clock out →CLOSED |  04:00 next day →AUTO_CLOSED

A session carries the plan, the readiness snapshot, the task timers, and the closing review. One session per local day is the norm; a second resume after clock-out is permitted and appends a segment rather than opening a new session, so split shifts and evening work do not fragment the record.

Clock in — 90 second budget

Three readiness fields, then the plan. Nothing else is ever added to this screen.

  1. Hours slept last night (stepper, prefilled from the last value)
  2. Energy 1–5, Stress 1–5 (two taps)
  3. Planned clock-out time (prefilled from the rolling median of the last 14 sessions)
  4. Today's one outcome, then 1–3 supporting tasks with Minimum / Target / Stretch tiers

The capacity verdict renders live as tasks are added, because the engine runs in the browser. A plan that exceeds the session window cannot be committed (REQ-032).

During the session

Exactly one task may be active. Starting a second stops the first. The header shows time remaining in the session and adjusted demand remaining, both counting down. When remaining demand exceeds remaining time by more than 20%, Rescue offers itself once — not repeatedly.

Clock out — 60 second budget

  1. Confirm or correct actual minutes on at most three items (slider, prefilled from timers)
  2. For anything unfinished, one tap from a fixed reason list — this is the diagnostic payload
  3. Tomorrow's one thing (optional, one line)

On close the session is sealed: plan, actuals and reasons become immutable, and calibration, drift and adaptation recompute.

Failure mode to design for: the user forgets to clock out. Auto-close at 04:00 local marks closedBy:"auto", discards timer tails beyond the last observed activity, and excludes the session from calibration — an unreviewed session must never poison the personal multiplier.

Clock out and seal

P0
Trigger
User taps Clock out on an OPEN session
Input
Confirmed actual minutes (max 3 prompts, prefilled from timers); one reason per unfinished item from the fixed list; optional tomorrow line
Compute
Seal session (immutable), then recompute calibration (§06.2), habit drift (§06.4) and the rolling weekly aggregate

State CLOSED, closedBy:"user"; one-screen summary showing planned vs actual as a single bar, no red

Clock out with zero completed work is a valid, non-judgemental outcome and still collects a reason

REQ-023

Auto-close

P0
Trigger
Cron, hourly: any OPEN session whose local time has passed 04:00 of the following day
Compute
Close open segments at the last heartbeat; do not credit unlogged time
Output
State AUTO_CLOSED; reviewed:false; excluded from calibration and from completion-rate statistics

Next clock-in offers a 20-second retro-review of the auto-closed session, once, skippable

Resume after clock out

P1
Trigger
User clocks in again on a date that already has a CLOSED session
Compute
Append a segment to the existing session; do not create a second session or a second plan
Output
Session reopens as OPEN; the sealed plan is unchanged, additional actuals attach to it
Done when
Split shifts produce one day record with two segments, and daily statistics count the day once

D · Planning & capacity

REQ-030

Minimum / Target / Stretch

P0
Trigger
Any task is created
Input
Three durations; Minimum is required, and defaults to 25% of Target
Compute
Capacity demand uses Target. Readiness band LOW substitutes Minimum for every task in the day's ceiling
Output
Completion at Minimum counts as a completed task, not a partial failure
Done when
A LOW-readiness day can be fully completed at Minimum tier and reports 100% completion
REQ-032

Capacity check blocks the commit

P0
Trigger
Commit pressed on a day or week plan
Input
Planned items with Target estimates; session window or 168h week; profile fixed blocks
Compute
available = window - fixed - buffer(12% day / 15% week) demand = SUM(target_i) * personalMultiplier §06.2 verdict = available - demand
Output
verdict < 0 → commit blocked. Shown: overage in h:mm, the sentence "You are scheduling X into Y", and the cut list. User cuts or defers until verdict ≥ 0
Edge
demand < 50% available → non-blocking "underplanned" note; never blocks
Edge
No calibration data (n < 5) → prior multiplier 1.40, and the multiplier is labelled "estimated"
Done when
An over-capacity plan cannot be committed by any path, and the overage is always stated in hours and minutes
Not doing
Deciding what to cut
REQ-034

If–then plan on obstacle

P1
Trigger
A task is unfinished twice, or the user names an obstacle
Compute
AI call site 3 (§07). Structured output: {cue, response}
Output
Stored on the task, surfaced at the moment the cue occurs, never in a digest
Done when
Every generated intention names an observable cue, not a feeling

E · Execution & rescue

REQ-040

What next

P0
Trigger
Today screen, no task active
Compute
Deterministic ranking function §06.5 over the committed plan only
Output
One task, with the one-line reason it won ("fits your remaining 40 minutes; due Thursday")
Edge
Nothing fits the remaining block → offer the Minimum tier of the top-ranked item
Done when
The same inputs always produce the same recommendation and the reason is always shown
REQ-041

Rescue mode

P0
Trigger
Remaining adjusted demand exceeds remaining session time by >20%. Offered once per session
Compute
Rank by §06.5, fill the remaining window greedily at Minimum tier, defer the remainder
Output
A revised schedule for the rest of the session and an explicit "moved to tomorrow" list. The original plan stays sealed and is not rewritten
Edge
Declining rescue never re-prompts that session
Done when
Rescue changes what is surfaced, and the recorded plan-versus-actual gap remains intact for review
REQ-042

Lapse re-entry

P0
Trigger
Clock in after 3+ consecutive days with no session
Output
A single screen: no backlog, no statistics, no streak language. One question — "what is the one thing for today?" Deferred items stay deferred and silent
Compute
Capacity ceiling for the first returning day is set to 60% of computed available
Done when
Returning after two weeks away shows fewer than 12 words of history

F · Habits

REQ-050

Drift detection, not streaks

P1
Trigger
Habit logged on clock-in or clock-out
Compute
Least-squares slope over the last 14 observations of deviation-from-target, in minutes §06.4
Output
Slope > 4 min/day and mean deviation > 20 min → state the drift in plain numbers and ask for one reason from a fixed list

Write path

Client form→Server action→Zod parse→Engine (pure)→Mongo write→revalidatePath

The same engine call runs client-side during editing to render the capacity verdict live. Server-side it runs again as the authority — the client result is a preview and is never trusted.

Connection caching. Cache the Mongoose connection promise on globalThis. Without it, dev hot reload opens a new connection per module reload and Atlas rate-limits the project within an hour.

Cron is not optional. Prompts and cues are one of the load-bearing mechanisms in this design, and Next.js has no scheduler. Auto-close (REQ-023) in particular is a correctness requirement, not a nicety — without it, forgotten sessions corrupt calibration.

05Data model

Three modelling decisions carry the whole schema. Get these wrong and the product cannot compute its own value proposition.

  1. One node collection, not five. Year through task in a single collection with a materialised ancestor path. Rollups and ancestor chains each become one indexed query; five collections make every rollup a five-way join, which is what Mongo is worst at.
  2. Estimates are written once. estimateTarget is never overwritten. Actual minutes live in a separate field. Calibration is impossible otherwise.
  3. Sealed sessions are immutable. The gap between plan and actual is the product; a mutable plan erases its own evidence.

  profile: { sleepTargetH, workH, commuteH, mealsH, lifeH }

Timezone rule. Every timestamp is UTC. Every daily-scoped document also carries localDate as a plain string in the user's timezone. "Today" is a calendar question, and UTC ranges break across DST and travel.

06The engine

Six pure functions. Every formula below is inspectable in the UI, because a number the user cannot audit is a number they will stop believing.

6.1 capacity
capacity({window, fixed, items, multiplier, utilization, readinessBand})
  buffer     = window * 0.12
  available  = (window - fixed - buffer) * utilization * bandFactor
  demand     = SUM(item.estimateTarget) * multiplier
  verdict    = available - demand        < 0 blocks the commit

6.2 calibration — shrinkage toward a global prior
calibrate(ratios)
  r_i        = actualMin_i / estimateTarget_i        completed, non-suspect
  personal   = exp( mean( ln r_i ) )                 geometric: ratios, not sums
  n          = min(count, 40)
  k          = 12                                    prior strength
  multiplier = exp( (n*ln(personal) + k*ln(1.40)) / (n + k) )
  clamp [0.8, 3.0]
  n=0 → 1.40 exactly. n=12 → halfway to personal. n=40 → 77% personal.

6.3 readiness
readiness({sleepH, sleepTargetH, energy, stress, yesterdayLoad})
  sleepScore    = clamp01(sleepH / sleepTargetH) * 100
  energyScore   = energy * 20
  stressScore   = (6 - stress) * 20
  recoveryScore = yesterdayLoad <= 1 ? 100 : clamp01(2 - yesterdayLoad) * 100
  score = .35*sleep + .30*energy + .20*stress + .15*recovery
  band  = score >= 75 ? HIGH(x1.00) : score >= 50 ? MEDIUM(x0.80) : LOW(x0.55)

6.4 drift
drift(observations[14])                              deviation from target, minutes
  slope = leastSquaresSlope(dayIndex, deviationMin)
  alert = slope > 4 AND |mean(deviation)| > 20
  reports "drifting ~7 min later per day, now 41 min past target"

6.5 ranking — what next
score(task, ctx) =
    0.30 * goalWeight/5
  + 0.25 * 1/(daysUntilDue + 1)
  + 0.20 * (adjEstimate <= blockRemaining ? 1 : 0.2)
  + 0.15 * energyFit(task.demand, ctx.band)
  - 0.10 * (task.goalId !== ctx.currentGoalId ? 1 : 0)     switch cost

6.6 adaptation — proportional controller, target 80%
adapt(utilization, completionRate)
  u' = clamp(u + 0.5*(completionRate - 0.80), 0.50, 0.95)
  delta capped at +/-0.15 per week

Two details worth defending. Calibration uses a geometric mean because these are ratios — an arithmetic mean lets one 5× overrun dominate permanently. And it shrinks toward 1.40 rather than starting at 1.0, so the app is useful on day one and converges on the individual by roughly week three.

Adaptation is a proportional controller aimed at 80% completion, correcting half the error each week. Full correction oscillates; half converges.

07AI boundary

Five call sites. Language in, or language out — never arithmetic, never a ranking, never a score.

Never AI

Capacity, calibration, readiness, drift, ranking, rescue scheduling, adaptation, and every percentage displayed anywhere in the product. These are §06.

The constraint on call site 4

The narrative receives only the computed stats object and is instructed to use no figure that is not in it. Output is validated: any number appearing in the prose that is absent from the payload fails the response and falls back to numbers-only. If the app invents one statistic, every real one loses its authority.

Three hard rules

  • No AI call is on a write path's critical line. Every site has a working non-AI fallback, because a model outage must never prevent someone clocking in.
  • All structured output is parsed through Zod before touching the database. A model response is untrusted input.
  • AI output is always presented as a draft that the user edits before it is saved.

At these volumes a small model for sites 1–3, one weekly paragraph, and batched embeddings land at roughly 4 cents per user per month. Putting a chat box on every screen instead costs dollars and makes worse decisions.

The gate between phases 2 and 3 is the one that matters. If the plan → execute → review loop does not hold its author for three weeks, no additional module will fix that. If it does, personal usage identifies the next module far more reliably than this document can.

09Kill list

Written down so it stops being reconsidered. Adding anything here requires deleting something shipped.

  • Streaks and gamification. Seductive, and it makes a missed day a reason to quit rather than a data point.
  • A chat assistant. Costs more, decides worse, and turns a 90-second screen into an open-ended conversation.
  • Nutrition, caffeine, alcohol, steps, strength-versus-cardio. None of it changes a planning recommendation. Principle 5.
  • Self-rated skill percentages. Users cannot rate themselves and the number would drive real decisions.
  • A social feed, sharing, accountability partners. A different product.
  • Infinite news scroll. Directly self-defeating for an execution system.
  • Full OKR modelling, key results, confidence scoring. A team methodology imported into a single-player tool.
  • Dopamine scores, productivity scores, any invented metric. Every displayed number must be traceable to user input.
  • Native mobile apps. Responsive web, installable, until phase 4 at the earliest.

10Open questions

  1. Calendar integration timing. Capacity is only as honest as its fixed-block input, and self-reported commitments are unreliable. Read-only Google Calendar in phase 2 would replace estimates with observation — at the cost of an OAuth flow before the loop is proven. Leaning: phase 3.
  2. Is the binding constraint hours or attention? The capacity model assumes hours. For most knowledge work the real ceiling is two to three deep-work blocks regardless of hours available. A second ceiling on deep-work count may be needed alongside the minutes ceiling.
  3. Should calibration segment by task type? Coding and admin almost certainly have different multipliers, but segmenting triples the data needed to leave the prior. Leaning: global in v1, segment once n > 100.
  4. Auto-close at 04:00 — right boundary? It works for a normal schedule and mis-attributes for night shifts. Possibly derived per user from observed clock-in times after two weeks.
  5. Notification channel. Web push has poor iOS reliability; the evening nudge is load-bearing for the loop. Email fallback is unglamorous but works.
